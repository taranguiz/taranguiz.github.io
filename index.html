I'm a Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Colorado Boulder. I'm a tectonic geomorphologist interested in how faults, climate, erosion, and sediment transport shape Earth's surface — combining field observations, remote sensing, geochronology, thermochronology, and numerical modeling to understand how landscapes respond to tectonic and climatic forcing.

Research themes

Active faults & landscape response

Slowly slipping faults leave measurable records in topography, drainage networks, sediment deposits, and surface deformation.

Climate, sediment & landscape evolution

Numerical models help test how climate variability and sediment cover influence river dynamics, divide migration, and fault-zone morphology.

Long-term exhumation & mountain evolution

Thermochronology and geologic constraints reveal cooling, exhumation, basin development, and landscape evolution over millions of years.

After completing my Ph.D. research in the hyperarid Atacama Desert, I'm expanding this work toward glaciated and postglacial landscapes, sediment routing, and tectonic geomorphology in mountain systems.

Methods

  • Field geology and geomorphic mapping
  • Satellite remote sensing, InSAR, GNSS, and topographic analysis
  • Geochronology, thermochronology, and sediment records
  • Landscape-evolution modeling and scientific programming
